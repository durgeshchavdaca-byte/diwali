Why the flame always points up

Tilt a diya any way you like. The flame still rises.

Our elders said that is the nature of the atman. Knocked down, turned around, it still reaches upward. Whatever this year bent in your life, may it find its way back up.

Five nights, five lessons

Diwali is not one night. It is a five-day lesson in how to live.

  1. Dhanteras day one

    We buy a little gold or a new vessel, and pray to Dhanvantari, the healer. Health is the first wealth. Everything else is counted after it.

  2. Naraka Chaturdashi day two

    Krishna defeats Narakasura, who held others captive. Before the new year, set free what holds you back. Old grudges, old fears, old pending work.

  3. Lakshmi Puja day three

    Homes are cleaned and lamps line every door, because Lakshmi is said to enter only where there is light and order. Prosperity is invited, not chased.

  4. Govardhan Puja · Bestu Varas day four

    Krishna lifts a mountain to shelter a whole village. In Gujarat it is also our New Year, Saal Mubarak. Real strength is the shelter you give others.

  5. Bhai Dooj day five

    Yamuna feeds her brother Yama, and even the lord of death leaves blessed. In the end, our people are the only balance sheet that matters.

A note from my desk

Here in Gujarat, on Lakshmi Puja, traders close the old chopda and open a new one. On its first page they write two words, side by side: शुभ लाभ. Shubh, then Labh. Goodness first, then gain.

I have spent my working life inside account books, and I have not found a better business philosophy than those two words, in that order. The numbers that last are the ones earned the right way.

Krishna said it in the Gita: कर्मण्येवाधिकारस्ते मा फलेषु कदाचन. Your right is to the work, never to its fruit. Do the work well, and leave the rest to Ma Lakshmi.

Thank you for letting us be a small part of your work this year. May your new chopda have more shubh, more labh, and fewer surprises. And if anything is weighing on you before the year closes, message me. Festival or not.

“Is this gift taxable?”

From family: no tax

Gifts from relatives are tax-free at any value: parents, siblings, spouse, children and the other relatives the law lists.

From others: over ₹50,000

Gifts from friends or anyone else become taxable once the year’s total crosses ₹50,000, and then the whole amount is taxed.
